# Teaching With & About AI

### A 4-Lesson Professional Development Series for Educators

A practical, low-jargon series that moves teachers from "AI is mysterious" to
"AI is something I can teach *about* and use *with* my students responsibly."
No coding, no accounts required to participate — just a willingness to try.

**The takeaway:** You don't need to be a technologist to give students a clear,
honest mental model of AI and a responsible-use habit that lasts.

---

## Who This Is For

- **Classroom teachers** (any subject, grades K–12) who want to address AI in class.
- **Instructional coaches / librarians** building AI-literacy across a school.
- **School leaders** shaping an AI-use policy without panic or bans.

**Prerequisites:** None. Bring a device if you have one; all activities work on paper too.

---

## What Participants Will Learn

1. **Demystify AI** in plain language and spot where it already shows up in school life.
2. **Explain generative AI** well enough to teach a student (data → pattern → prediction).
3. **Integrate AI** into their own subject ethically and with learning goals first.
4. **Coach responsible use** — citations, verification, privacy, and academic integrity.

---

## Learning Objectives

| # | Objective | Bloom's Level | Lesson |
|---|-----------|---------------|--------|
| 1 | **Define** AI and distinguish it from ordinary software using 2 criteria | Remember / Understand | 1 |
| 2 | **Identify** at least 4 places AI already touches students' lives | Understand | 1 |
| 3 | **Explain** how a generative AI produces text/images (training data, pattern matching, prediction) | Understand | 2 |
| 4 | **Recognize** 3 common failure modes (hallucination, bias, opacity) and why they matter for learners | Understand / Analyze | 2 |
| 5 | **Apply** one AI tool to a real task in their subject area with a learning goal stated first | Apply | 3 |
| 6 | **Evaluate** an AI-integration idea for fairness, accuracy, and student agency | Evaluate | 3 |
| 7 | **Design** a classroom activity that teaches responsible AI use (cite, verify, protect privacy) | Create | 4 |
| 8 | **Formulate** a short, teachable "AI Citizen" agreement for their students | Create | 4 |

## Series Outline

### Lesson 1 — What Is AI (and Where Is It Already in School)?

**Duration:** ~60 min · **Objectives:** 1, 2

**Purpose:** Replace "AI = magic/robot takeover" with a calm, useful definition
and surface the AI students already meet daily.

**Timing & flow**

| Segment | Activity | Time |
|---------|----------|------|
| Hook | "Is This AI?" sort: 12 cards (spell-check, Netflix recs, calculator, ChatGPT, a thermostat, a worksheet…) → group debate edge cases | 10 min |
| Concept | Plain definition: a system that **learns patterns from data** to make predictions/decisions. Contrast with fixed rules (recipe vs. examples). | 15 min |
| Map | "AI in Our Students' Day" brainstorm → cluster into categories (vision, language, prediction, recommendation). | 15 min |
| Close | Pair share: name one AI myth you'll stop repeating to students. Exit ticket. | 20 min |

**Facilitator moves:** Keep it concrete; avoid math/CS jargon. Use the
spell-check vs. ChatGPT contrast to land "learns from examples."

**Participant activity:** Exit ticket — "In one sentence, what is AI? + one place you didn't realize it was already in your classroom."

**Formative assessment:** Card-sort accuracy + exit ticket (checked for the 2-criteria definition).

---

### Lesson 2 — How Generative AI Works (Enough to Teach It)

**Duration:** ~60 min · **Objectives:** 3, 4

**Purpose:** Give teachers a correct, kid-friendly mental model of generative AI
and the honest limits that keep students safe.

**Timing & flow**

| Segment | Activity | Time |
|---------|----------|------|
| Recap | One-word check-in: the biggest surprise from Lesson 1. | 5 min |
| Model | "Predict the next word" live demo: facilitator types a sentence, shows token-by-token prediction. Tie to autocorrect. | 15 min |
| Mechanics | Three plain steps: (1) trained on huge text/data, (2) finds patterns, (3) predicts the most likely next piece. Not "thinking," not "knowing." | 15 min |
| Limits | Demo a **hallucination** live (ask about a fictional event/person). Name 3 failure modes: hallucination, bias from data, opacity ("can't explain why"). | 15 min |
| Close | "Explain it to a student" 1-min rehearsal with a partner. | 10 min |

**Facilitator moves:** The hallucination demo is the emotional anchor — make it
real, not theoretical. Emphasize *verify, don't trust*.

**Participant activity:** Write a 3-sentence "explain generative AI to a 12-year-old" and trade for peer feedback.

**Formative assessment:** Peer feedback on the 3-sentence explanation (checks objective 3).

---

### Lesson 3 — Using AI in Your Subject (Ethically)

**Duration:** ~60 min · **Objectives:** 5, 6

**Purpose:** Move from understanding to practice — one real, learning-first use
of AI in each teacher's subject, screened for fairness and agency.

**Timing & flow**

| Segment | Activity | Time |
|---------|----------|------|
| Warm-up | Share-out: one repetitive task in your role AI could help with. | 10 min |
| Hands-on | Guided try: use a free AI tool (e.g., a public chatbot) on a **real** task — draft a rubric, generate quiz questions, brainstorm a lab, summarize a reading. Learning goal stated *first*. | 20 min |
| Screen | "Fairness & Agency" checklist applied to your idea: Does it preserve student thinking? Is it accurate? Is it equitable? | 15 min |
| Plan | Each teacher outlines one classroom activity using AI, with a stated learning objective. | 15 min |

**Facilitator moves:** Push "learning goal first, tool second." Flag any idea
where the AI does the student's thinking for them.

**Participant activity:** Draft a one-page "AI integration plan" for one upcoming unit.

**Formative assessment:** Plan reviewed against the Fairness & Agency checklist (objectives 5 + 6).

---

### Lesson 4 — Teaching Responsible AI Use (The AI Citizen)

**Duration:** ~60 min · **Objectives:** 7, 8

**Purpose:** Turn the series into student-facing practice — a teachable
responsible-use habit and a class agreement.

**Timing & flow**

| Segment | Activity | Time |
|---------|----------|------|
| Reflect | Gallery walk of Lesson 3 plans; sticky-note one risk each. | 10 min |
| Framework | Introduce the **3 commitments**: **Cite** AI help · **Verify** outputs · **Protect** privacy. | 10 min |
| Build | Small groups design a 20-min responsible-use lesson for their grade band (script + handout). | 20 min |
| Close | Each group shares; co-author a one-paragraph "AI Citizen Agreement" for the school. Sign-off. | 20 min |

**Facilitator moves:** Keep the agreement short and student-owned. Tie privacy
back to Lesson 2's "opacity" limit.

**Participant activity:** Draft the responsible-use lesson + contribute to the AI Citizen Agreement.

**Summative assessment:** Responsible-use lesson plan (objective 7) + the co-authored agreement (objective 8), both reviewed by facilitator for completeness and age-appropriateness.

10-min break between each pair.

## Self-Check for Facilitators (after delivering all 4)

| Criteria | Not yet (1) | Developing (3) | Confident (5) |
|----------|-------------|---------------|---------------|
| **Mental model** | "It's magic" | "I can describe it roughly" | "I can teach it to a student" |
| **Practice** | "Haven't tried it" | "Tried it once for me" | "Designed a student activity" |
| **Safety** | "Trust the output" | "Sometimes verify" | "Cite, verify, protect by habit" |
| **Agency** | "Ban or allow, all-or-nothing" | "Case-by-case" | "Goal-first, tool-second" |
