# 4-Week Introduction to Artificial Intelligence — Grade 8

**Audience:** Grade 8 students (ages 13–14), no prior programming or AI experience.
**Duration:** 4 weeks, ~3 hours/week (12 contact hours).
**Goal:** Build conceptual AI literacy — what AI is, how it learns, where it shows up, and how to use it responsibly.

---

## Week 1 — What Is AI?

**Learning objectives**
- Define artificial intelligence in student-friendly language and distinguish it from "regular" software.
- Identify examples of AI in daily life (recommendations, filters, voice assistants, games).
- Explain the difference between rule-based systems and systems that learn from data.

**Lessons / activities**
- *Day 1 (1 hr):* Kickoff — "Is this AI?" sorting game. Students classify apps/tools as AI or not and debate edge cases.
- *Day 2 (1 hr):* Core concepts — intelligence, automation, machine "learning" vs. hard-coded rules. Analogies: recipe (rules) vs. learning from examples (ML).
- *Day 3 (1 hr):* AI Around Us — scavenger hunt: list AI they used before school. Group share + map to categories (vision, language, prediction).

**Assessment:** Exit-ticket quiz (5 multiple-choice) + 1-paragraph "Explain AI to a 5th grader."

---

## Week 2 — How Machines Learn

**Learning objectives**
- Describe the basic idea of training data, patterns, and prediction.
- Explain supervised learning using a concrete example (e.g., spam vs. not-spam).
- Recognize that biased or incomplete data leads to biased outputs.

**Lessons / activities**
- *Day 1 (1 hr):* Training data — build a "classifier" by hand: given labeled examples of cats vs. dogs, derive rules.
- *Day 2 (1 hr):* Bias & fairness — short demo: train a classifier on skewed data, observe unfair results. Discuss real-world stakes.
- *Day 3 (1 hr):* Hands-on — use a no-code ML playground (e.g., Teachable Machine) to train a simple image/gesture model.

**Assessment:** Reflection worksheet — "What data trained my model, and what could go wrong?"

---

## Week 3 — AI in the Real World

**Learning objectives**
- Categorize AI applications across domains (health, art, transport, education).
- Describe at least one ethical concern (privacy, jobs, misinformation, deepfakes).
- Evaluate a claim about AI critically rather than accepting it.

**Lessons / activities**
- *Day 1 (1 hr):* Domain tour — jigsaw reading: each group researches one sector, presents impacts (good + worrying).
- *Day 2 (1 hr):* Deepfakes & misinformation — watch examples, practice spotting synthetic media.
- *Day 3 (1 hr):* Ethics debate — structured debate: "Should schools use AI to grade essays?"

**Assessment:** Group poster + 1-min pitch on an AI application and its biggest risk.

---

## Week 4 — Using AI Responsibly & Creatively

**Learning objectives**
- Use a generative AI tool to produce a small creative or research artifact.
- Apply a personal "responsible use" checklist (cite AI, verify facts, protect privacy).
- Articulate one way they'll use AI as a learner going forward.

**Lessons / activities**
- *Day 1 (1 hr):* Prompting basics — craft effective prompts; compare vague vs. specific outputs.
- *Day 2 (1 hr):* Create — use an AI tool to draft a short story, infographic, or study guide (teacher-supervised).
- *Day 3 (1 hr):* Showcase & wrap — present work; co-create a class "AI Citizen Pledge."

**Assessment:** Final project (AI-assisted artifact + 1-page reflection on how they used AI responsibly) + class pledge signed.

---

## Resources
- Teachable Machine (Google) — no-code ML training
- Short curated videos on deepfakes / recommender systems (teacher-selected)
- Printed "AI Citizen Pledge" template
- Optional extension: a guest talk from a local technologist

## Bloom's Alignment
Remember/Understand (Wk1) → Apply/Analyze (Wk2–3) → Create/Evaluate (Wk4).
