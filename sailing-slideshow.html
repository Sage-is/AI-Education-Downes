Sailing

From Shore to Open Water

A gentle introduction

Why Sail?

  • Silent, wind-powered travel
  • Connection with weather and water
  • A skill that scales from dinghies to ocean crossings

Anatomy of a Sailboat

  • Hull — the body that floats
  • Mast — vertical spar holding the sails
  • Main sail & jib — catch the wind
  • Rudder — steers the boat
  • Keel — prevents sideways drift

How a Boat Sails

Sails act like wings. Wind flowing over the curved surface creates lift, pulling the boat forward. A keel balances that lift so you move ahead instead of sideways.

Points of Sail

  • In irons — straight into the wind (no go)
  • Close-hauled — as close to the wind as possible
  • Beam reach — wind from the side (fast!)
  • Running — wind from behind

Core Knots

  • Bowline — a fixed loop that won't slip
  • Cleat hitch — secure a line to a cleat
  • Figure-eight — stop a line from running out

Safety First

  • Wear a life jacket
  • Check the weather and tide
  • Tell someone your float plan
  • Know how to right a capsized boat

Getting Started

  1. Take a beginner course at a local club
  2. Practice on a small dinghy
  3. Sail with experienced crew
  4. Build toward longer coastal trips

Fair Winds

Every sailor starts on the shore. The wind is waiting.
