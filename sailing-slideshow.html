Why Sail?

  • Silent, wind-powered travel — no engine, no fuel
  • A direct connection with weather and water
  • A skill that scales: dinghies to ocean crossings
  • Slow travel that rewards attention over speed

Anatomy of a Sailboat

  • Hull — the body that floats
  • Mast & boom — spars that hold the sails
  • Main sail & jib — catch and shape the wind
  • Rudder — steers the boat
  • Keel / centerboard — resists sideways drift

How a Boat Actually Sails

Sails act like wings. Wind flowing over the curved surface creates lift, pulling the boat forward. The keel balances that sideways lift so you drive ahead instead of sliding sideways.

Sailboats don't just get pushed — they convert wind into forward thrust, even at angles to it.

Points of Sail

  • In irons — straight into the wind (no-go zone)
  • Close-hauled — as close to the wind as possible
  • Beam reach — wind from the side (fastest point)
  • Broad reach — wind from behind-quarter
  • Running — wind dead astern

Changing Direction

  • Tacking — turning through the wind (to go upwind)
  • Gybing — turning away from the wind (downwind)
  • Upwind progress is made in a series of tacks, zig-zagging

You can't sail straight into the wind — so you work around it.

Core Knots

  • Bowline — a fixed loop that won't slip
  • Cleat hitch — secure a line to a cleat
  • Figure-eight — stop a line from running out
  • Reef knot — join two lines of equal size

Reading the Wind

  • Watch flags, ripples, and the tell-tales on your shrouds
  • Look for "cat's paws" — streaks of breeze on the water
  • Wind shifts are opportunities: sail the lifts, avoid the headers

Safety First

  • Always wear a life jacket
  • Check weather and tides before leaving
  • File a float plan with someone ashore
  • Know how to right a capsized dinghy
  • Keep a charged phone or VHF radio aboard

Getting Started

  1. Take a beginner course at a local sailing club
  2. Learn on a small, forgiving dinghy
  3. Sail with experienced crew
  4. Build gradually toward longer coastal trips

Fair Winds
