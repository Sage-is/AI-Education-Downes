# Assessments with Rubrics: Grade 9 English Learning Objectives

Below are assessments designed and aligned to core Grade 9 English learning objectives, each with a detailed rubric.

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension & Literary Analysis

**Learning Objective:** Analyze how authors develop themes, characters, and literary devices in fiction and nonfiction texts.

### Assessment: Literary Analysis Essay
**Task:** Read a short story (e.g., "The Lottery" by Shirley Jackson) and write a 3–5 paragraph essay analyzing how the author uses **symbolism, foreshadowing, and irony** to develop the central theme.

### Rubric (20 points)

| Criteria | Exemplary (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (1–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thesis & Claim** | Clear, insightful thesis directly addresses theme and devices | Clear thesis addresses theme and at least 2 devices | Thesis is present but vague or only partially addresses the prompt | No clear thesis or off-topic |
| **Textual Evidence** | 3+ well-chosen quotes, seamlessly integrated and thoroughly explained | 2–3 relevant quotes with adequate explanation | 1–2 quotes with limited or surface-level explanation | No quotes or evidence is irrelevant |
| **Analysis Depth** | Insightful connections between devices and theme; goes beyond the obvious | Solid analysis linking devices to theme | Some analysis present but mostly summary or description | Little to no analysis; relies on retelling |
| **Organization & Structure** | Logical flow with clear intro, body, and conclusion; effective transitions | Organized with intro, body, conclusion; some transitions | Loosely organized; missing or weak transitions | Disorganized; hard to follow |

## 2. Argumentative Writing

**Learning Objective:** Construct well-supported arguments using claims, counterclaims, evidence, and reasoning.

### Assessment: Argumentative Essay
**Task:** Write a 5-paragraph argumentative essay on a debatable topic (e.g., "Should social media be banned for users under 16?"). Must include a claim, at least one counterclaim with rebuttal, and evidence from at least 2 sources.

### Rubric (25 points)

| Criteria | Exemplary (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (1–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim & Focus** | Precise, compelling claim maintained throughout | Clear claim maintained with minor lapses | Claim present but unfocused or shifts | No discernible claim |
| **Evidence & Sources** | 3+ pieces of credible evidence from multiple sources, properly cited | 2 pieces of relevant evidence with citations | 1 piece of evidence or sources are weak/uncited | No evidence or fabricated sources |
| **Counterclaim & Rebuttal** | Acknowledges strongest counterclaim and dismantles it with logic/evidence | Addresses a counterclaim with reasonable rebuttal | Mentions a counterclaim but rebuttal is weak | No counterclaim addressed |
| **Reasoning & Logic** | Flawless logical connections; no fallacies | Sound reasoning with minor gaps | Some logical gaps or unsupported leaps | Reasoning is unclear or contains fallacies |
| **Conventions & Style** | Virtually error-free; formal academic tone throughout | Few errors; mostly formal tone | Several errors that occasionally impede clarity | Frequent errors that impede understanding |

## 3. Vocabulary & Language Use

**Learning Objective:** Determine and apply the meaning of unfamiliar words using context clues, word roots, and reference materials; demonstrate command of figurative language.

### Assessment: Vocabulary in Context Portfolio
**Task:** Given 10 passages containing underlined unfamiliar words, students must: (a) identify the meaning using context clues, (b) identify the root/affix and its origin, (c) use each word correctly in an original sentence, and (d) identify 3 examples of figurative language across the passages and explain their effect.

### Rubric (20 points)

| Criteria | Exemplary (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (1–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Context Clue Application** | 9–10 words correctly defined with clear reasoning about which clues were used | 7–8 words correctly defined | 5–6 words correctly defined | Fewer than 5 correct |
| **Word Roots & Affixes** | Accurately identifies roots/affixes for 9–10 words with etymological detail | Accurately identifies roots for 7–8 words | Identifies roots for 5–6 words, some inaccuracies | Fewer than 5 or mostly inaccurate |
| **Original Sentences** | All sentences demonstrate precise, nuanced understanding of meaning | Most sentences show correct usage | Some sentences show correct usage; others are awkward or incorrect | Most sentences misuse the word |
| **Figurative Language** | 3 examples identified and effects explained with depth and textual support | 3 examples identified with adequate explanation | 2 examples identified or explanations are shallow | Fewer than 2 or explanations missing |

## 4. Speaking & Listening

**Learning Objective:** Present claims and findings clearly, using appropriate eye contact, volume, and multimedia; engage in collaborative discussions with evidence-based contributions.

### Assessment: Socratic Seminar + Oral Presentation
**Task:** (Part A) Participate in a 20-minute Socratic seminar on a class novel. (Part B) Deliver a 3–5 minute individual presentation arguing for the novel's most important theme, supported by textual evidence and at least one visual aid.

### Rubric (20 points)

| Criteria | Exemplary (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (1–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Seminar Participation** | 4+ substantive contributions that build on others' ideas; cites text | 3 contributions that reference the text | 1–2 contributions with limited textual support | Silent or off-topic contributions |
| **Presentation Content** | Clear thesis, compelling evidence, logical argument structure | Clear thesis with adequate evidence | Thesis present but evidence is thin or disorganized | No clear thesis or evidence |
| **Delivery & Presence** | Confident eye contact, clear projection, natural pace, engages audience | Good eye contact, audible, steady pace | Inconsistent eye contact, occasionally hard to hear | Reads directly from notes; inaudible or rushed |
| **Visual Aid / Multimedia** | Visual enhances understanding; creative and well-designed | Visual supports the argument adequately | Visual is present but loosely connected to content | No visual or visual is distracting/irrelevant |

## 5. Research & Informational Writing

**Learning Objective:** Conduct short research projects; gather relevant information from multiple credible sources; synthesize findings into a coherent informational text with proper citations.

### Assessment: Research-Based Informational Report
**Task:** Research a topic related to a current unit (e.g., the impact of the Harlem Renaissance on modern literature). Produce a 2–3 page informational report using at least 4 credible sources with in-text citations and a Works Cited page (MLA format).

### Rubric (25 points)

| Criteria | Exemplary (5) | Proficient (4) | Developing (3) | Beginning (1–2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Research Quality** | 4+ credible, varied sources (academic, primary, reputable); demonstrates thorough investigation | 4 credible sources with some variety | 2–3 sources, some of questionable credibility | Fewer than 2 sources or sources are unreliable |
| **Synthesis & Organization** | Information from sources is seamlessly synthesized into a coherent narrative with clear sections | Information is organized with some synthesis across sources | Mostly organized but reads as source-by-source summary | Disorganized; information is copied or listed |
| **Content Accuracy & Depth** | Factually accurate; provides insightful analysis beyond surface-level reporting | Accurate; adequate depth | Mostly accurate with some shallow treatment | Contains inaccuracies or is superficial |
| **MLA Citations** | Flawless in-text citations and Works Cited page | Minor citation errors (1–2) | Several citation errors or inconsistent formatting | No citations or Works Cited, or pervasive errors |
| **Writing Conventions** | Virtually error-free; sophisticated sentence variety and academic register | Few errors; appropriate register | Some errors; informal tone intrudes | Frequent errors that impede comprehension |

### Implementation Notes
- **Differentiation:** Each rubric can be adapted by adjusting the point thresholds or providing scaffolded templates (graphic organizers, sentence starters) for developing learners.
- **Formative checkpoints:** Build in draft reviews, peer feedback sessions, and self-assessment using the rubric before the final submission for each assessment.
- **Student-facing rubrics:** Share rubrics with students at the time of assignment so expectations are transparent and students can self-monitor quality.
