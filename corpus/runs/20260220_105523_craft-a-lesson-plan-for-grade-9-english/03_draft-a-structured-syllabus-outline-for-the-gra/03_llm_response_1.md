# Grade 9 English — Structured Syllabus Outline

---

## Course Overview

This Grade 9 English course develops students' critical reading, analytical writing, oral communication, and language skills through the study of diverse literary and informational texts. The curriculum is aligned to Common Core ELA standards and emphasizes higher-order thinking, evidence-based reasoning, and effective communication.

**Target Audience:** Grade 9 students (ages 14–15)
**Duration:** Full academic year (~36 weeks)
**Modules:** 6 core modules

---

## Learning Objectives (Course-Level)

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Analyze literary and informational texts for theme, structure, and craft
2. Write coherent, evidence-based arguments, narratives, and expository essays
3. Conduct short and sustained research projects using credible sources
4. Participate in collaborative discussions with preparation and accountability
5. Apply standard English conventions in writing and speaking
6. Evaluate the impact of author's choices on meaning and audience

---

## Module 1: The Power of Narrative (Weeks 1–6)

**Focus:** Short fiction, personal narrative, and narrative craft

**Description:** Students explore how authors construct narratives to convey meaning. Through close reading of short stories from diverse voices, students analyze elements such as point of view, conflict, characterization, and theme. They apply these techniques in their own narrative writing.

**Key Texts (suggested):**
- "The Most Dangerous Game" — Richard Connell
- "Thank You, Ma'am" — Langston Hughes
- "The House on Mango Street" (excerpts) — Sandra Cisneros
- Selected contemporary short stories

**Aligned Objectives:** 1, 5, 6

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Close Reading Protocols** | Annotation-based reading with text-dependent questions to build analytical habits |
| **Writer's Workshop** | Drafting, peer review, and revision cycles for personal narratives |
| **Think-Pair-Share** | Short collaborative discussions to unpack story elements before whole-class dialogue |
| **Mentor Text Analysis** | Students study published narratives as models for their own writing craft |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: Reading journals, annotation checks, exit tickets
- Summative: Personal narrative essay (3–5 pages) + literary analysis paragraph

---

## Module 2: Argument & Persuasion (Weeks 7–12)

**Focus:** Argumentative reading and writing, rhetoric, and evidence evaluation

**Description:** Students examine how writers and speakers build arguments. They identify claims, evidence, reasoning, and rhetorical appeals (ethos, pathos, logos) in mentor texts, speeches, and media. The module culminates in a formal argumentative essay on a relevant issue.

**Key Texts (suggested):**
- "Letter from Birmingham Jail" (excerpts) — Martin Luther King Jr.
- Selected op-eds and editorials (current, age-appropriate)
- TED Talks and speech transcripts
- Informational articles on student-selected issues

**Aligned Objectives:** 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Socratic Seminar** | Student-led discussions on debatable topics grounded in textual evidence |
| **Claim-Evidence-Reasoning (CER) Framework** | Structured approach to constructing and evaluating arguments |
| **Gallery Walk** | Students analyze posted arguments/ads for rhetorical strategies |
| **Debate Protocols** | Structured mini-debates to practice oral argumentation and counterargument |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: Rhetorical analysis graphic organizers, CER practice paragraphs
- Summative: Argumentative essay (5 paragraphs, MLA format) + brief oral defense

---

## Module 3: The Novel Study — Identity & Society (Weeks 13–18)

**Focus:** Extended text analysis, theme development, and literary interpretation

**Description:** Students engage in a deep study of a full-length novel exploring themes of identity, belonging, and society. They trace character development, analyze symbolism, and connect themes to broader human experiences. Literature circles promote collaborative inquiry.

**Key Texts (choose 1–2):**
- *To Kill a Mockingbird* — Harper Lee
- *The Absolutely True Diary of a Part-Time Indian* — Sherman Alexie
- *A Raisin in the Sun* — Lorraine Hansberry
- *Inside Out & Back Again* — Thanhha Lai

**Aligned Objectives:** 1, 4, 5, 6

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Literature Circles** | Small-group roles (discussion director, passage picker, connector, illustrator) rotate weekly |
| **Dialectical Journals** | Double-entry journals connecting textual evidence to personal response and analysis |
| **Fishbowl Discussion** | Inner/outer circle format for modeling and observing high-quality literary discussion |
| **Scaffolded Essay Writing** | Step-by-step instruction through thesis development, outlining, drafting, and revision |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: Weekly literature circle participation, dialectical journal entries, reading quizzes
- Summative: Literary analysis essay (thesis-driven, 4–6 paragraphs) on a thematic question

---

## Module 4: Poetry & Figurative Language (Weeks 19–24)

**Focus:** Poetry analysis, figurative language, sound devices, and creative expression

**Description:** Students explore poetry across eras and cultures, analyzing how poets use language, structure, and imagery to create meaning and emotional impact. Students practice writing original poems and presenting spoken word performances.

**Key Texts (suggested):**
- Poems by Langston Hughes, Maya Angelou, Robert Frost, Emily Dickinson
- Contemporary poets: Amanda Gorman, Naomi Shihab Nye, Billy Collins
- Spoken word/slam poetry videos (Button Poetry, etc.)

**Aligned Objectives:** 1, 5, 6

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **TP-CASTT Analysis** | Title, Paraphrase, Connotation, Attitude, Shifts, Title (revisit), Theme — structured poetry analysis |
| **Creative Writing Workshop** | Guided poetry writing using specific forms and techniques (free verse, sonnet, haiku) |
| **Performance-Based Learning** | Students rehearse and deliver spoken word or poetry recitations |
| **Comparative Analysis** | Side-by-side examination of poems with shared themes but different styles |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: TP-CASTT analyses, poetry journal, peer feedback on drafts
- Summative: Poetry portfolio (3–5 original poems + artist's statement) + spoken word performance

---

## Module 5: Research & Informational Literacy (Weeks 25–30)

**Focus:** Research skills, source evaluation, informational writing, and digital literacy

**Description:** Students develop research competencies by investigating a self-selected topic within a guided framework. They learn to locate credible sources, evaluate bias, synthesize information, and present findings in a structured research report.

**Key Resources:**
- Library databases and curated online sources
- "They Say / I Say" templates (Graff & Birkenstein) for academic writing
- Fact-checking and lateral reading guides

**Aligned Objectives:** 2, 3, 4, 5

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Inquiry-Based Learning** | Students develop their own research questions within a broad thematic frame |
| **CRAAP Test for Sources** | Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose — framework for evaluating credibility |
| **Mini-Lessons + Conferences** | Direct instruction on skills (paraphrasing, citation) paired with individual writing conferences |
| **Peer Review Workshops** | Structured feedback sessions using rubric-aligned checklists |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: Annotated bibliography (5+ sources), outline, rough draft checkpoints
- Summative: Research report (1,500–2,000 words, MLA format) + works cited page

---

## Module 6: Drama, Media & Synthesis (Weeks 31–36)

**Focus:** Dramatic literature, media literacy, and cumulative skill application

**Description:** Students study a dramatic work, analyzing how dialogue, stage directions, and structure convey conflict and theme. They also examine media texts (film, advertisements, digital media) for rhetorical and persuasive techniques. The course concludes with a synthesis project demonstrating cumulative learning.

**Key Texts (suggested):**
- *Romeo and Juliet* or *A Midsummer Night's Dream* — William Shakespeare
- Film adaptation clips for comparative analysis
- Selected media/advertising texts

**Aligned Objectives:** 1, 2, 4, 5, 6

**Instructional Strategies:**
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| **Readers' Theater / Performance** | Students perform scenes to deepen comprehension of dramatic structure and character |
| **Film vs. Text Comparison** | Analytical viewing guides to compare directorial and authorial choices |
| **Media Literacy Deconstruction** | Students analyze advertisements and digital content for bias, audience, and persuasive techniques |
| **Capstone Project (Choice Board)** | Students choose a culminating product: multimedia presentation, creative adaptation, analytical essay, or podcast |

**Assessments:**
- Formative: Scene performance, media analysis short responses, project checkpoints
- Summative: Capstone synthesis project + reflection essay

## Cross-Cutting Instructional Practices

These strategies are embedded throughout all modules:

- **Differentiation:** Tiered texts, flexible grouping, scaffolded graphic organizers, and choice in assessment format
- **Vocabulary Development:** Academic and domain-specific vocabulary instruction using Frayer models and contextual learning
- **Grammar in Context:** Grammar and conventions taught through student writing (not isolated drills)
- **Formative Assessment Loops:** Regular use of exit tickets, self-assessments, and check-ins to adjust instruction
- **SEL Integration:** Journaling, collaborative norms, and texts that invite personal connection and empathy

This syllabus provides a balanced, standards-aligned framework that progresses from narrative and argumentative foundations through research, poetry, and drama — building student competency across all ELA strands while allowing teacher flexibility in text selection and pacing.
