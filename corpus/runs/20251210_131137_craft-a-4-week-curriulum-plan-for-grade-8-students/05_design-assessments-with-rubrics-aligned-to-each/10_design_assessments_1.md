## Assessments & Rubrics

Aligned assessments for each learning objective:
### Assessment 1: Reflection
**Aligned Objective:** 1. Define plagiarism and co-creation in the context of AI-generated content, articulating accurate distinctions in a short paragraph.  
**Assessment Summary:** Students compose a short paragraph (100-150 words) defining plagiarism and co-creation in AI contexts, clearly articulating distinctions between them. 

| Level     | Descriptor                                                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Beginning | Definitions are incomplete or inaccurate; no clear distinction between concepts. |
| Developing| Basic definitions provided; partial distinction with minor inaccuracies.   |
| Proficient| Accurate definitions with clear, precise distinctions supported by context. |
| Advanced  | Nuanced definitions with insightful distinctions and relevant AI examples. |
- **Skills Demonstrated:** Accurate recall and articulation of key terms; ability to synthesize distinctions in writing.  
- **Evidence of Mastery:** Paragraph includes precise definitions, explicit contrasts, and error-free articulation.  
- **Feedback Focus:** Clarity of distinctions, terminology accuracy, and depth of contextual understanding.  

### Assessment 2: Quiz
**Aligned Objective:** 2. Identify examples of plagiarism versus ethical co-creation in sample AI-assisted texts, classifying at least five instances with 90% accuracy.  
**Assessment Summary:** Online quiz presents 6-8 sample AI-assisted text excerpts; students classify each as plagiarism or ethical co-creation with justifications.  

| Level     | Descriptor                                                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Beginning | Fewer than 50% accurate classifications; justifications absent or irrelevant. |
| Developing| 50-74% accuracy; justifications present but vague or inconsistent.         |
| Proficient| 75-89% accuracy; justifications align with ethical criteria consistently.  |
| Advanced  | 90%+ accuracy; justifications demonstrate deep analysis of ethical nuances. |
- **Skills Demonstrated:** Recognition of plagiarism indicators; application of classification criteria to examples.  
- **Evidence of Mastery:** High accuracy rate with detailed, context-specific justifications for 90%+ of instances.  
- **Feedback Focus:** Precision in classification, quality of justifications, and handling of edge cases.  

### Assessment 3: Presentation
**Aligned Objective:** 3. Explain the ethical risks of unattributed AI use, referencing two real-world scenarios in a brief oral presentation.  
**Assessment Summary:** Students deliver a 3-5 minute recorded oral presentation explaining ethical risks of unattributed AI, citing two specific real-world scenarios.  

| Level     | Descriptor                                                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Beginning | Risks vaguely mentioned; no real-world scenarios or inaccurate references. |
| Developing| Basic risks explained; one scenario referenced with limited detail.        |
| Proficient| Clear explanation of risks; two accurate scenarios with relevant connections. |
| Advanced  | Insightful risk analysis; two scenarios deeply integrated with implications. |
- **Skills Demonstrated:** Oral communication of ethical concepts; integration of real-world evidence.  
- **Evidence of Mastery:** Logical structure linking risks to two verified scenarios with clear verbal delivery.  
- **Feedback Focus:** Depth of risk explanation, scenario relevance, and presentation clarity/engagement.  

### Assessment 4: Project
**Aligned Objective:** 4. Differentiate acceptable AI co-creation from plagiarism in a given writing task, producing a revised artifact with proper attribution.  
**Assessment Summary:** Students receive a draft writing task with embedded AI content, revise it to differentiate ethical co-creation from plagiarism, and include attribution statements.  

| Level     | Descriptor                                                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Beginning | Revisions minimal; plagiarism uncorrected, attribution absent or incorrect. |
| Developing| Partial revisions; some differentiation with inconsistent attribution.     |
| Proficient| Clear differentiation; plagiarism removed, proper attribution for co-creation. |
| Advanced  | Sophisticated revisions; exemplary attribution with rationale for choices. |
- **Skills Demonstrated:** Critical editing for ethics; application of attribution practices in artifacts.  
- **Evidence of Mastery:** Revised artifact shows precise differentiation and transparent, standardized attribution.  
- **Feedback Focus:** Accuracy of revisions, attribution completeness, and ethical decision-making transparency.  

### Assessment 5: Reflection
**Aligned Objective:** 5. Develop a personal ethical guideline for AI collaboration, outlining three rules with justifications for beginner-level application.  
**Assessment Summary:** Students create a 300-400 word reflective document outlining three personal rules for ethical AI collaboration, with justifications tailored to beginners.  

| Level     | Descriptor                                                                 |
|-----------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Beginning | Fewer than three rules; justifications superficial or absent.              |
| Developing| Three rules listed; justifications basic and beginner-oriented.            |
| Proficient| Three clear rules with solid, practical justifications for beginners.      |
| Advanced  | Three innovative rules with comprehensive, evidence-based justifications.  |
- **Skills Demonstrated:** Synthesis of ethics into personal guidelines; justification for practical application.  
- **Evidence of Mastery:** Rules are actionable for beginners, supported by logical, context-specific rationales.  
- **Feedback Focus:** Rule originality, justification strength, and beginner-level accessibility.

**Assessment Criteria Template:**

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Accuracy/Correctness | 40% |
| Clarity/Communication | 30% |
| Application/Transfer | 30% |
