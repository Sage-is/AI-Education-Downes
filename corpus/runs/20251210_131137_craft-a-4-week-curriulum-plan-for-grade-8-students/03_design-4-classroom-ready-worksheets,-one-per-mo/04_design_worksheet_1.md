# Worksheet: Module 1: Understanding Plagiarism in AI Contexts

- **Audience**: Grade 8 students
- **Skill focus**: Recognizing and defining plagiarism when using AI-generated content
- **Estimated time**: 30 minutes
- **Materials**: Pencil, notebook

## Warm-Up Prompt
Let's kick things off by thinking about what you've heard of plagiarism before—now with a twist involving AI tools!

1. In your notebook, jot down one idea of what plagiarism means to you in everyday writing.
2. Imagine using an AI tool like ChatGPT to write a story: How might copying its output directly into your homework count as plagiarism? Explain in 1-2 sentences.

## Guided Practice
Build your skills by exploring a clear definition of plagiarism and spotting it in simple AI scenarios.

1. Write a one-sentence definition of plagiarism in the context of AI tools (hint: it's using someone else's—or something else's—ideas without proper credit).
2. Read these examples and circle **Y** for yes (plagiarism) or **N** for no:

| Scenario | Y/N |
|----------|-----|
| Copying an AI-generated essay word-for-word as your own |     |
| Rewording an AI suggestion and citing "AI tool helped with ideas" |     |
| Submitting AI art as your hand-drawn picture |     |

3. For one "Y" example, explain why it's plagiarism in your own words.

## Apply & Create
Put your understanding into action by analyzing real-life cases and making your own example.

1. Read: "Sara asked an AI for poem ideas, pasted three lines unchanged into her assignment, and didn't mention the AI." Is this plagiarism? Why or why not—write 2-3 sentences.
2. Create your own quick scenario (2-3 sentences) where a student uses AI ethically (no plagiarism).
3. Swap with a partner (if possible) and discuss: Does theirs avoid plagiarism? Note one strength.

## Reflection & Exit Ticket
Take a moment to celebrate what you've learned and check your mastery.

1. In 1-2 sentences, explain the key difference between using AI help ethically and plagiarizing.
2. Exit ticket: Define plagiarism with AI in one sentence, and name one example from today.

## Differentiation
- **All Learners**: Follow tasks as written with partner discussions for support.
- **Needs Support**: Provide sentence starters (e.g., "Plagiarism is when...") and pre-highlight one example in Guided Practice #2.
- **Ready for More**: Add a task to research and share one real-world consequence of AI plagiarism (e.g., in journalism).

## Answer Key
- **Warm-Up Prompt**: 1. Ex: Copying without credit. 2. Ex: It's not your original work; AI generated it.
- **Guided Practice**: 1. Ex: Using AI-generated content as your own without credit. 2. Y, N, Y. 3. Ex: Direct copy steals the AI's output.
- **Apply & Create**: 1. Yes—unchanged lines without credit. 2. Ex: Student uses AI for brainstorming, rewrites fully, cites AI. 3. Student feedback varies; look for credit/rewriting emphasis.
- **Reflection & Exit Ticket**: 1. Ex: Ethical = rewrite + credit AI; plagiarism = copy without credit. 2. Matches Guided Practice #1; references any "Y" example.
