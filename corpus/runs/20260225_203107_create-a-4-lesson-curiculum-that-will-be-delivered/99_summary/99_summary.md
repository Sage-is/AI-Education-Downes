# Maker-Mindedness in the Age of AI Magic

## What You'll Learn

- **What "maker-mindedness" means** and why understanding *how* things work matters more than ever
- **How consumer AI platforms can trick us** into feeling powerful while actually taking away our real skills and agency
- **Practical strategies** to stay in the driver's seat when using AI tools—using them as tools, not letting them use you

---

## About This Course

Join our new short video series, designed to help young people navigate a world where AI tools like ChatGPT, image generators, and coding assistants promise to do "magic" with just a prompt. But here's the catch: when AI does the thinking for you, who's really in control? This course empowers students to stay curious, creative, and capable—maintaining real agency by understanding the difference between *using* a tool and *depending* on one.

---

## Course Outline

### 4 Lessons · 4 Reflection Activities · 1 Final Project

---

### Lesson 1: The Magic Trick — How AI Creates the Illusion of Effortless Power

**Duration:** 12-14 minutes

**Description:**  
Students explore what happens when AI tools make complex tasks look instant and easy. Using relatable examples (AI-generated art, homework help, code generators), this lesson introduces the concept of "magic as a mask"—and why convenience might come at a cost.

**Key Concepts:**
- Consumer AI platforms and their promises
- The "black box" problem: inputs and outputs without understanding
- The difference between *using* a tool and *understanding* a tool
- Real agency vs. the feeling of agency

**Video Structure:**
1. Hook: A "magic trick" demo using an AI image generator (30 sec)
2. The setup: What consumer AI platforms promise (2 min)
3. The reveal: What's hidden behind the curtain (3 min)
4. Discussion prompt: "When was the last time you felt like you 'did' something without knowing how?" (1 min)
5. Core concept: Agency means understanding, not just results (3 min)
6. Reflection activity intro (2 min)

**Activity — "The Black Box Detective":**
- [ ] Choose one AI tool you use (or have heard of)
- [ ] Answer: What does it do? What does it need from you? What does it hide from you?
- [ ] Write 3 questions you'd need answered to truly understand how it works

---

### Lesson 2: What Is Maker-Mindedness? (And Why You Already Have It)

**Duration:** 10-12 minutes

**Description:**  
This lesson introduces the maker mindset—a way of approaching the world with curiosity, hands-on exploration, and a desire to understand *how* and *why* things work. Students discover they've been "makers" their whole lives, and learn why this mindset is their superpower in an AI world.

**Key Concepts:**
- Definition of maker-mindedness: curiosity, iteration, hands-on learning, understanding systems
- The maker's question: "How does this work, and can I change it?"
- Real-world examples of maker thinking (coding, crafting, tinkering, DIY)
- Why maker-mindedness builds real skills and real agency

**Video Structure:**
1. Hook: A montage of kids making things—Lego, baking, coding, drawing (30 sec)
2. Definition: What is maker-mindedness? (2 min)
3. Contrast: Maker mindset vs. consumer mindset (2 min)
4. Your maker history: You've been doing this forever (2 min)
5. Why it matters now: AI can't replace your understanding (2 min)
6. Reflection activity intro (1 min)

**Activity — "My Maker Portfolio":**
- [ ] List 5 things you've made, fixed, or figured out in the last year
- [ ] For each, note: What did you learn? What would an AI not understand about it?
- [ ] Identify one "maker skill" you want to develop further

---

### Lesson 3: The Agency Trap — When Convenience Becomes Dependence

**Duration:** 12-14 minutes

**Description:**  
Students examine the risks of over-relying on AI tools. Through scenarios and discussion, they explore how "outsourcing thinking" can erode skills, creativity, and confidence—and learn to recognize when a tool is helping vs. when it's replacing them.

**Key Concepts:**
- The agency spectrum: tool use → tool reliance → tool dependence
- Warning signs: "I can't do this without AI"
- Case studies: Writing, coding, and creative work
- Stanford HAI's warning: "convincing but incorrect outputs" and "motivation crises" from AI over-reliance **[Verified]**

**Video Structure:**
1. Hook: A scenario—two students, same project, different approaches (1 min)
2. The agency spectrum explained (2 min)
3. Case study 1: AI for writing—helper or replacement? (2 min)
4. Case study 2: AI for coding—what happens when the AI is wrong? (2 min)
5. Research insight: What experts say about AI and motivation **[Verified source: Stanford HAI]** (2 min)
6. Reflection activity intro (1 min)

**Activity — "Agency Audit":**
- [ ] Track your AI use for 2 days
- [ ] For each use, categorize: Tool (I'm in control) / Crutch (AI is doing my thinking) / Collaboration (We're working together)
- [ ] Identify one area where you want to shift from "crutch" to "tool" or "collaboration"

---

### Lesson 4: Staying in the Driver's Seat — Your Maker's Toolkit for the AI Age

**Duration:** 14-15 minutes

**Description:**  
The final lesson equips students with practical strategies to maintain agency while using AI tools. They'll learn how to question, verify, iterate, and extend AI outputs—turning AI from a "magic genie" into a "collaborator."

**Key Concepts:**
- The "maker's questions" for AI outputs: Is this correct? How could I improve it? What's missing?
- Verification strategies: cross-checking, testing, iterating
- Using AI to *learn*, not just to *produce*
- Building a personal "maker's toolkit" for the AI age

**Video Structure:**
1. Hook: The "AI Co-Pilot" metaphor—who's flying? (30 sec)
2. Strategy 1: Question everything—The 3 Questions Framework (2 min)
3. Strategy 2: Verify, don't trust—Practical checks (2 min)
4. Strategy 3: Iterate—Use AI as a starting point, not a finish line (2 min)
5. Strategy 4: Extend—Add your own thinking, creativity, and expertise (2 min)
6. Final project intro: "The Maker's Manifesto" (2 min)

**Activity — Final Project: "The Maker's Manifesto":**
- [ ] Create a personal guide (poster, video, or slideshow) answering:
  - What does maker-mindedness mean to me?
  - How will I use AI tools without losing my agency?
  - What's one skill I commit to developing "the hard way"?
- [ ] Share with peers or family for feedback

| **Critical AI Awareness** | Identifies specific risks and strategies with nuance | Recognizes key risks and proposes strategies | Surface-level awareness; generic strategies | Little to no critical awareness shown |
| **Commitment to Agency** | Specific, actionable commitment with rationale | Clear commitment with some specificity | Vague or generic commitment | No clear commitment |
| **Creativity & Presentation** | Engaging, polished, and appropriate for chosen format | Clear and well-organized | Basic presentation; some organization issues | Unclear or incomplete |

## Key Insights from Verified Sources

> - Models producing "convincing but incorrect outputs"
> - Potential for "student motivation crises" when AI does the work
> - Need for "developing students' AI literacy" as a priority
> - Importance of maintaining human judgment and critical thinking
