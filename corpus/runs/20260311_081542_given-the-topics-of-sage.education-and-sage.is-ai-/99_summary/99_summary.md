# AI with a Maker Mindset
### A Hands-On Curriculum Powered by Sage.Education & Sage.is AI-UI

**What you'll learn in 3 points:**
- How to use AI tools like Sage.Education and Sage.is AI-UI as a *creator*, not just a consumer
- How to think critically, iterate rapidly, and build real AI-powered solutions
- How to apply the maker mindset — curiosity, experimentation, and reflection — to navigate an AI-rich world ethically and confidently

---

## About This Course

Join this immersive, project-based course designed to help you *make things with AI* — not just use it. Grounded in the **maker mindset** (rooted in Carol Dweck's growth mindset research and the maker movement's ethos of hands-on creation) and powered by the open-source, privacy-first platforms **Sage.Education** and **Sage.is AI-UI**, this curriculum treats AI as a creative collaborator, not a replacement for human thinking.

Every module combines **conceptual grounding**, **structured workshops**, and **open-ended making time**. Learners design prompts, build AI agents, interrogate outputs, reflect on ethics, and ship real projects.

---

## Who Should Join?

### Target Audience
- Middle and high school students (core audience)
- Educators seeking to integrate AI into their classroom practice
- Library staff, makerspace facilitators, and instructional coaches
- School teams piloting Sage.Education or Sage.is AI-UI

### Prerequisites
- Basic computer literacy (typing, browser navigation)
- Curiosity and willingness to experiment
- No prior AI or coding experience required

## Course Outline

**8 Modules · 24 Sessions · 12+ Workshops · 8 Signature Projects**

---

### Module 1: What Is AI, Really?
**2 Sessions | Theme: Demystification**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Define AI, machine learning, and large language models (LLMs) in plain language
- [ ] Distinguish between AI as a tool vs. AI as a decision-maker
- [ ] Identify examples of AI in everyday life

**Sessions:**
1. **Session 1 — AI Everywhere:** Gallery walk + sorting activity (AI vs. not AI)
2. **Session 2 — Inside the Black Box:** How LLMs predict text; demo with Sage.Education

**Workshop 1: "Stump the Bot"**
- Teams craft questions designed to confuse, mislead, or expose the limits of an AI (LLM Chatbot)
- Debrief: What does the AI *not* know? Why? What does that tell us about how it works?

**Signature Project Seed:** Begin a personal "AI Field Journal" — observations, surprises, questions

---

### Module 2: The Maker Mindset Meets AI
**3 Sessions | Theme: Creator Identity**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Articulate the core values of the maker mindset (curiosity, iteration, collaboration, resilience)
- [ ] Contrast the role of *AI creator* vs. *AI consumer*
- [ ] Connect growth mindset principles (Dweck) to AI experimentation

**Sessions:**
3. **Session 3 — Makers Make Things:** Intro to maker culture, Maker Faire history, maker values
4. **Session 4 — You Are Not a Passive Consumer:** Using Sage.Education to observe *how* AI responds, not just *what* it says (learning visibility feature)
5. **Session 5 — Fail Forward:** Rapid iteration cycles with AI; celebrating "useful failures"

**Workshop 2: "Make It Yours" — Custom Prompt Crafting**
- Using Sage.Education's prompt editor, learners write 3 system prompts for different personas (a tutor, a devil's advocate, a creative collaborator)
- Swap and test each other's prompts; debrief what worked and why

**Workshop 3: "Iteration Sprint"**
- Given a real problem (e.g., *"How do I explain photosynthesis to a 7-year-old?"*), teams iterate on a prompt 5 times, tracking changes and improvements
- Document the iteration chain in their Field Journal

**Signature Project 1:** "My First AI Agent" — design a custom chatbot using Sage.Education for a personal or classroom use case

---

### Module 3: Talking to AI — The Art & Science of Prompting
**3 Sessions | Theme: Communication as Craft**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Apply prompt engineering techniques (role, context, format, constraints)
- [ ] Adapt prompting strategies across grade levels and subject areas
- [ ] Evaluate prompt quality based on output quality

**Sessions:**
6. **Session 6 — Anatomy of a Prompt:** Role · Task · Context · Format · Constraints framework
7. **Session 7 — Prompting for Different Goals:** Creative writing vs. research vs. problem-solving
8. **Session 8 — Grade-Level Prompting:** Adapting language complexity using Sage.Education's resources

**Workshop 4: "Prompt Remix"**
- Start with a weak prompt; teams compete to improve it in 3 rounds
- Judge outputs on accuracy, usefulness, and creativity

**Workshop 5: "The Prompt Library"**
- Collaborative activity: teams contribute their best prompts to a shared Sage.is AI-UI Community Hub collection
- Peer review and rating of contributions

**Activity: Prompt Postcards**
- Each learner designs a "Prompt Postcard" — a beautifully formatted card with their best prompt, the output it generated, and a reflection on what made it work

---

### Module 4: Understanding LLMs — How They Think (and Don't)
**3 Sessions | Theme: Critical Literacy**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Explain how LLMs generate responses (pattern matching, training data, temperature)
- [ ] Identify common failure modes: hallucination, bias, outdated knowledge
- [ ] Apply critical evaluation strategies to AI outputs

**Sessions:**
9. **Session 9 — Training Data & Bias:** Where does the model's "knowledge" come from?
10. **Session 10 — Hallucination Nation:** Why AI confidently gets things wrong
11. **Session 11 — Fact-Checking AI:** Cross-referencing outputs with authoritative sources

**Workshop 6: "Bias Hunt"**
- Teams prompt the same LLM with variations on sensitive topics (gender, geography, history)
- Document patterns; present findings to the class
- Debrief: *What is the model reflecting back to us about our world?*

**Workshop 7: "Hallucination Detective"**
- Feed AI a set of questions with verifiable answers; identify and flag incorrect responses
- Discuss: When is hallucination dangerous? When is it creative?

**Signature Project 2:** "The AI Reliability Report" — a one-page analysis of an AI tool's strengths, weaknesses, and appropriate use cases

---

### Module 5: Building with Sage — Agents, RAG & Knowledge Bases
**3 Sessions | Theme: Platform Mastery**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Navigate Sage.Education and Sage.is AI-UI interfaces confidently
- [ ] Create a custom AI agent with a defined role and knowledge base
- [ ] Use RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) to ground AI responses in specific documents

**Sessions:**
12. **Session 12 — Sage Platform Tour:** Sage.Education AI Labs + Sage.is AI-UI features (multi-model chat, team spaces, Community Hub)
13. **Session 13 — Building a Knowledge Base:** Uploading documents; testing RAG-powered responses
14. **Session 14 — Agent Design Sprint:** Defining agent purpose, persona, and constraints

**Workshop 8: "Agent Arcade"**
- Teams have 60 minutes to build a functional AI agent for a real audience (younger students, parents, the school library)
- Demo and feedback session: does the agent behave as designed?

**Workshop 9: "Privacy by Design"**
- Compare Sage.is (self-hosted, open-source) vs. commercial AI platforms
- Discuss: *Why does it matter where your data goes?*
- Teams audit their agents for privacy considerations

**Signature Project 3:** "The Expert Agent" — a fully deployed AI agent with a custom knowledge base serving a real school or community need

---

### Module 6: AI Ethics & the Responsibility of the Maker
**2 Sessions | Theme: Values in Action**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Apply ethical frameworks to AI design and use decisions
- [ ] Examine the difference between commercial AI priorities and educational/human-centered values
- [ ] Articulate a personal AI ethics stance

**Sessions:**
15. **Session 15 — Who Benefits? Who's Left Out?:** Examining AI's social impact
16. **Session 16 — Human-Centered AI Design:** Sage's philosophy of elevating human insight, not replacing it

**Workshop 10: "Ethics Tribunal"**
- Scenario-based role play: teams argue for and against AI use in three contested cases (grading essays, medical diagnosis, creative writing)
- Verdict + written reflection

**Activity: "My AI Pledge"**
- Each learner writes a personal commitment statement: *How will I use AI in a way that reflects my values?*
- Displayed in the classroom / shared on Sage Community Hub

---

### Module 7: Making Things — Capstone Project Sprint
**4 Sessions | Theme: Build, Ship, Reflect**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Apply all course learning to an original, maker-driven AI project
- [ ] Practice iterative design: plan → prototype → test → refine → present
- [ ] Give and receive structured peer feedback

**Sessions:**
17. **Session 17 — Project Ideation:** Brainstorm real problems worth solving with AI; choose a direction
18. **Session 18 — Prototype Day 1:** Build a working prototype using Sage tools
19. **Session 19 — Prototype Day 2 + Peer Feedback:** Test with real users; incorporate feedback
20. **Session 20 — Polish & Prepare:** Finalize project; create a presentation or demo

**Workshop 11: "Maker Fair Prep"**
- Teams prepare a 5-minute demo showcasing their project: *What problem does it solve? How does it work? What did you learn from failure?*
- Practice with constructive peer critique using a structured feedback protocol (Warm / Cool / Wonder)

**Signature Projects 4–8 (choose one or combine):**
- [ ] **The AI Tutor:** Build a subject-specific tutoring agent for a real class
- [ ] **The Community Tool:** Solve a local problem (school, library, neighborhood) with an AI agent
- [ ] **The AI Zine:** Create a print/digital zine documenting your AI learning journey with prompts, reflections, and critiques
- [ ] **The Bias Audit:** Conduct a rigorous bias audit of a popular AI tool and publish findings
- [ ] **The Maker Manifesto:** Write, design, and publish a human-centered AI manifesto for your school

---

### Module 8: Showcase, Reflect & What's Next
**4 Sessions | Theme: Celebration & Continuity**

**Objectives:**
- [ ] Present final projects to an authentic audience
- [ ] Reflect on personal growth and remaining questions
- [ ] Map a personal path forward as an AI-literate maker

**Sessions:**
21. **Session 21 — Maker Showcase (Day 1):** Live demos and presentations
22. **Session 22 — Maker Showcase (Day 2):** Q&A, feedback, celebration
23. **Session 23 — Reflection Harvest:** Field Journal synthesis; *What do I know now that I didn't before?*
24. **Session 24 — The Road Ahead:** Emerging AI trends, open-source community participation, next steps

**Workshop 12: "Pass It On"**
- Each learner designs a 10-minute mini-lesson teaching one skill from the course to a new audience (younger students, parents, teachers)
- Final act of the maker: *Teaching is making.*
