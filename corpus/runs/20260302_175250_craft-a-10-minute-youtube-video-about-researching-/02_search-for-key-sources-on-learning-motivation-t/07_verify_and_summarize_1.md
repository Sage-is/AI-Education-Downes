### Summary
This page provides a comprehensive overview of Self-Determination Theory (SDT), a major framework for understanding human motivation that distinguishes between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation sources. The content details SDT's organismic dialectical approach, its six mini-theories (Cognitive Evaluation Theory, Organismic Integration Theory, Causality Orientations Theory, Basic Psychological Needs Theory, Goal Contents Theory, and Relationships Motivation Theory), and applications across domains including education, organizations, health, and parenting.

### Key Concepts
Self-Determination Theory, intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, autonomy, competence, relatedness, internalization continuum, basic psychological needs, organismic dialectical approach

### Relevance Assessment
SDT is one of the most influential and empirically supported motivation theories in educational psychology, directly informing how curriculum developers can design learning environments that foster engagement and persistence.
