# The Science of Small Wins

**Why Tiny Changes Create Big Results**

- 10-minute interactive session
- Practical strategies you can use today
- Based on behavioral science research

---

## Hook: The 1% Problem

Why do 92% of New Year's resolutions fail by February?

- We aim too big, too fast
- Willpower is a limited resource
- Our brains resist dramatic change

Small shifts bypass our brain's alarm system.

Notes: Ask the audience: "Who here has made a resolution and abandoned it?" Pause for hands. This creates immediate engagement and shared experience.

---

## Theory Overview: How Habits Form

**The Habit Loop:**

- **Cue** → Triggers the behavior
- **Routine** → The action itself
- **Reward** → Why your brain remembers

**Key insight:** Smaller routines require less motivation, making the loop easier to complete.

Notes: Draw the loop on a whiteboard if available. Emphasize that tiny actions slip under our brain's resistance radar.

---

## Research Evidence: What the Data Shows

**BJ Fogg, Stanford University:**
- Participants who started with "2-minute habits" had 85% success rate after 6 months

**James Clear's findings:**
- 1% improvements compound to 37x better results in one year

**Bottom line:** Consistency beats intensity every time.

Notes: These aren't abstract numbers—they represent real people who succeeded by starting small.

---

## Takeaways: Apply This Today

**Start ridiculously small:**
- Want to read more? Read one page
- Want to exercise? Do one push-up

**Stack habits:**
- Link new behavior to existing routines
- "After I brush my teeth, I will floss one tooth"

**Celebrate tiny wins:**
- Progress fuels motivation

Notes: Have participants choose one micro-habit right now. Give them 30 seconds to write it down.

---

## Call-to-Action: Your 2-Minute Challenge

**Right now, commit to one tiny habit:**

1. Pick something you've been avoiding
2. Shrink it until it takes less than 2 minutes
3. Anchor it to something you already do daily

**Share your micro-habit with someone nearby.**

*Small wins build momentum. Momentum builds change.*

Notes: Facilitate a 60-second pair share. Close by reminding them: "Your future self is built on today's smallest choices."
