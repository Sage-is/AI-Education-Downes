# Mouth Ulcers: Identify, Understand & Manage

A 10-minute introductory class on recognizing and addressing small oral ulcers (aphthous ulcers) that form where the gums meet the inner cheek.

### What You'll Learn

1. How to **identify** aphthous ulcers by their appearance, location, and symptoms
2. How to **distinguish** them from other oral conditions and know when to worry
3. How to **treat** minor ulcers at home and when to **seek professional care**

---

## About This Course

Join this short class designed to help you take confident, informed action the next time a painful mouth sore appears along your gum line. In just 10 minutes you'll move from "What is this sore?" to knowing exactly what to do about it — and when to see a dentist or doctor instead.

## Course Outline — 3 Modules · 4 Activities

### Module 1: What Is It? — Identification & Differentiation (3 min)

**Aligned to:** LO1, LO2

| Content | Instructional Strategy |
|---------|----------------------|
| Define aphthous ulcers; prevalence (~20% of adults) | Hook with a relatable clinical scenario |
| Visual characteristics: small (< 1 cm), round/oval, white-yellow center, red halo | Show annotated images or diagrams |
| Location: buccal mucosa at gum-cheek junction, soft palate, tongue | Label on oral anatomy diagram |
| **Differentiation table:** aphthous vs. cold sores vs. oral thrush (location, vesicles vs. patches, pain type) | Side-by-side comparison slide |

**Activity:** *"Spot the Difference"* — learners view 2–3 images and classify each as aphthous ulcer, cold sore, or thrush.

---

### Module 2: Why Does It Happen? — Causes & Prevention (3–4 min)

**Aligned to:** LO2, LO3

| Content                                                                                                                                               | Instructional Strategy                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Common causes: mechanical trauma (aggressive brushing, ill-fitting appliances), nutritional deficiencies (B12, iron, folate), stress, immune triggers | Cause-and-effect matching activity                                               |
| Risk factors: poor oral hygiene, acidic/spicy foods, SLS-containing toothpaste, tobacco                                                               | Brief discussion prompt                                                          |
| Prevention strategies: gentle brushing technique, balanced nutrition, stress management, regular dental visits                                        | *"Two Things You'll Change"* — learners mentally commit to two preventive habits |

---

### Module 3: What Do I Do? — Home Care & Red Flags (3 min)

**Aligned to:** LO3, LO4

| Content | Instructional Strategy |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Do's:** Saltwater rinse (½ tsp salt / 8 oz warm water), OTC topical analgesics (benzocaine gel), soft bland diet, OTC protective pastes | Live demonstration or short video clip |
| **Don'ts:** Spicy/acidic foods, picking at the ulcer, SLS toothpaste | Quick "myth vs. fact" callouts |
| **Red flags — seek care if:** Ulcer lasts > 2 weeks · Recurs frequently (>3×/year) · Unusually large (>1 cm) · Painless with hard/raised edges · Accompanied by fever or swollen lymph nodes | Red-bordered warning callout |
| Simple decision tree: Self-care → Monitor → Professional referral | Treatment algorithm slide |

**Activity:** *"What Would You Do?"* — 2 brief scenarios; learners choose self-care vs. seek professional help.

## Take-Home Worksheet — Spot the Signs & Plan Your Care

### Activity 1: Spot the Signs

| # | Sign or Symptom | Oral Ulcer? |
|---|----------------|-------------|
| 1 | Small, round sore with white/yellow center and red border inside the mouth | YES / NO |
| 2 | Painless, hard lump on the outer lip that does not change | YES / NO |
| 3 | Burning or tingling before a sore appears on the gums or inner cheek | YES / NO |
| 4 | Bright red, bleeding gums only when brushing | YES / NO |
| 5 | Pain while eating or talking due to an open sore on the tongue or soft palate | YES / NO |

1. _______________________________________________________________
2. _______________________________________________________________

### Activity 2: My Self-Care Action Plan

- [ ] Switch to an SLS-free toothpaste
- [ ] Keep saltwater rinse supplies at home
- [ ] Purchase an OTC oral analgesic gel
- [ ] Track ulcer occurrences in a journal (date, size, duration, possible trigger)
- [ ] Schedule a dental checkup if ulcers recur more than 3× per year
