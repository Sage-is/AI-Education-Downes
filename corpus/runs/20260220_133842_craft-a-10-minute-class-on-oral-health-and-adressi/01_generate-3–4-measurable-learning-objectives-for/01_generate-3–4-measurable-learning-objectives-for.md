**1. Identify aphthous ulcers by appearance and location**
> By the end of this class, learners will be able to **identify** aphthous ulcers by describing at least 3 visual characteristics (size, color, shape) and their common location along the gum-cheek junction.

**2. Differentiate aphthous ulcers from other oral lesions**
> By the end of this class, learners will be able to **distinguish** minor aphthous ulcers from at least 2 other common oral conditions (e.g., cold sores, oral thrush) based on key differentiating features such as location, pain pattern, and appearance.

**3. Apply appropriate self-care measures**
> By the end of this class, learners will be able to **select** at least 2 evidence-based home remedies or over-the-counter treatments (e.g., saltwater rinse, topical analgesics, avoiding irritating foods) to manage symptoms and promote healing of minor aphthous ulcers.

**4. Recognize warning signs that require professional evaluation**
> By the end of this class, learners will be able to **list** at least 3 red-flag indicators (e.g., ulcers lasting more than 2 weeks, recurring frequently, unusually large, accompanied by fever) that warrant seeking professional dental or medical evaluation.
