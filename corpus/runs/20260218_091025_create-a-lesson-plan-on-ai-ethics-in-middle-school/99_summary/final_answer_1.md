# AI Ethics: Being a Good Digital Citizen

## What You'll Learn
- **Define Artificial Intelligence:** Understand how AI differs from human decision-making.
- **Spot Bias:** Identify how AI can learn unfair behaviors from humans.
- **Protect Privacy:** Learn how to keep personal data safe in an AI-driven world.

---

## About this Lesson
Join our new introductory lesson, designed to help middle school students navigate the digital world responsibly. As AI becomes part of daily life—from social media feeds to smart home devices—it is crucial for young learners to understand the ethical implications of the technology they use every day. This lesson moves beyond "how it works" to "how it behaves," fostering critical thinking and digital citizenship.

## Who Should Join?
- **Target Audience:** Middle School Students (Ages 11–14).
- **Prerequisites:** No prior coding or technical experience required. Basic familiarity with apps and social media is helpful.

---

## Course Outline (1 Lesson・3 Activities)

### Lesson 1: AI Ethics Foundations
**Duration:** 45 Minutes

#### 1. Hook: Magic or Machine? (5 Minutes)
- **Activity:** Class discussion on everyday AI usage (Siri, TikTok, Netflix recommendations).
- **Goal:** Demystify AI as a "super-fast robot brain" rather than magic.

#### 2. Concepts: What is AI Ethics & The Copycat Problem (10 Minutes)
- **Direct Instruction:**
  - Define Ethics as "knowing the difference between right and wrong."
  - Explain **Bias**: How AI learns from human data and can repeat "bad words" or unfair patterns (The Parrot Analogy).
- **Goal:** Establish that AI is human-made and reflects human flaws.

#### 3. Activity: The Blue Shirt Team (15 Minutes)
- **Scenario:** A robot picks a kickball team but only selects kids wearing blue shirts.
- **Discussion Questions:**
  1. Is this fair?
  2. Why did the robot do this?
  3. How do we fix it?
- **Goal:** Students identify algorithmic bias and propose solutions (fixing data or instructions).

#### 4. Privacy Matters (10 Minutes)
- **Discussion:** How AI collects photos, chats, and locations.
- **Analogy:** Comparing personal data to a diary you wouldn't give to a stranger.
- **Goal:** Evaluate the trade-offs between convenience and privacy.

#### 5. Closing: Be a Tech Detective (5 Minutes)
- **Exit Ticket:** Students write one rule for a "Fairness Code" for school AI.
- **Goal:** Synthesize learning into a practical ethical guideline.

---

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. **Define Artificial Intelligence** by comparing human decision-making processes with computer algorithms using real-world examples (e.g., recommendation systems).
2. **Identify Potential Biases** in AI systems by analyzing a specific scenario (such as the "Blue Shirt Team" activity) to explain how it might impact different groups of people.
3. **Evaluate Trade-offs** between personal privacy and AI convenience by describing how a smart device collects data and proposing one method to protect user information.
4. **Distinguish Authentic Content** from AI-generated media (introductory concept) by applying a verification checklist to sample images.
5. **Formulate a "Code of Ethics"** for a fictional AI assistant by predicting three possible negative consequences and establishing rules to prevent them.

## Assessments & Rubrics

### Assessment 1: The Blue Shirt Team Discussion (Formative)
**Aligned Objective:** Identify Potential Biases
**Summary:** Students discuss the fairness of an algorithm that picks students based on shirt color.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Identifies the root cause (bad data/instructions) and proposes multiple viable solutions. |
| Meets Expectations | Identifies the unfairness and explains clearly why the robot acted that way. |
| Approaching Expectations | Identifies the unfairness but struggles to explain *why* the robot did it. |
| Below Expectations | Does not identify the scenario as unfair or unfair. |

### Assessment 2: Fairness Code Project (Summative)
**Aligned Objective:** Formulate a "Code of Ethics"
**Summary:** Students design a three-point "Fairness Code" for a hypothetical AI chatbot used in school, addressing honesty, privacy, or inclusivity.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Proposes three distinct, highly relevant rules with compelling justifications for complex ethical concerns. |
| Meets Expectations | Proposes three clear rules that correctly address specific ethical concerns (honesty, privacy, inclusivity). |
| Approaching Expectations | Proposes rules relevant to ethics but lacking specificity or distinct concerns. |
| Below Expectations | Fails to propose three rules or rules do not address ethical concerns. |
