## Assessments & Rubrics

Aligned assessments for each learning objective:
### Assessment 1: Reflection
**Aligned Objective:** Analyze a simulated social media feed to identify at least one example of algorithmic bias, providing a written explanation of how the bias might affect different users.
**Assessment Summary:** Students will engage with a pre-designed simulated social media feed containing clear indicators of algorithmic bias (e.g., gender or racial stereotyping in content suggestions). Students must write a reflective analysis identifying the bias and hypothesizing its impact on diverse user groups.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Identifies multiple examples of bias and provides a nuanced, comprehensive explanation of impacts on various user demographics. |
| Meets Expectations | Identifies at least one clear example of bias and explains its potential effects on different users logically. |
| Approaching Expectations | Identifies a potential bias but the explanation of its impact on users is vague, incomplete, or inaccurate. |
| Below Expectations | Fails to identify algorithmic bias or provides an explanation that does not address user impact. |

*   **Skills Demonstrated:** Critical thinking, social awareness, written analysis.
*   **Evidence of Mastery:** Ability to spot subtle patterns in algorithmic outputs and articulate social consequences.
*   **Feedback Focus:** Depth of analysis regarding the specific effects on different demographic groups.

### Assessment 2: Project
**Aligned Objective:** Create a visual 'data journey' map that traces how a specific piece of personal information (e.g., a photo or location) is collected by an AI system, accurately labeling three key stages of the process.
**Assessment Summary:** Students will select a piece of personal data and design a visual flowchart or infographic. This map must illustrate the lifecycle of the data from initial collection through processing to final usage or storage, highlighting the interaction with AI systems.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Creates a detailed, polished visual map with more than three key stages labeled accurately, showing deep insight into data flow. |
| Meets Expectations | Creates a clear visual map that accurately labels the three required key stages of the data collection process. |
| Approaching Expectations | Creates a visual map that labels the stages but contains inaccuracies in the flow or missing technical details. |
| Below Expectations | Visual map is incomplete, missing labels, or does not accurately represent the data collection process. |

*   **Skills Demonstrated:** Information literacy, visual communication, systems thinking.
*   **Evidence of Mastery:** Correct identification and sequencing of data lifecycle stages in a visual format.
*   **Feedback Focus:** Accuracy of the technical process and clarity of the visual presentation.

### Assessment 3: Quiz
**Aligned Objective:** Evaluate a set of paired images and text samples to distinguish between human-created and AI-generated content, achieving at least 80% accuracy on the identification task.
**Assessment Summary:** In a controlled digital environment, students will view a series of paired samples (images and text blocks). For each pair, they must determine which is human-created and which is AI-generated, requiring them to apply detection strategies learned in class.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Achieves 90-100% accuracy, consistently identifying subtle distinctions between human and AI content. |
| Meets Expectations | Achieves 80-89% accuracy, reliably distinguishing between human and AI content in most samples. |
| Approaching Expectations | Achieves 70-79% accuracy, showing some ability to distinguish but struggling with consistent identification. |
| Below Expectations | Achieves less than 70% accuracy, unable to reliably differentiate between human and AI content. |

*   **Skills Demonstrated:** Pattern recognition, critical evaluation, media literacy.
*   **Evidence of Mastery:** High percentage of correct identifications based on visual and textual cues.
*   **Feedback Focus:** Specific cues or artifacts missed during the evaluation process.

### Assessment 4: Presentation
**Aligned Objective:** Propose a 'Fairness Code' consisting of three rules for a hypothetical AI chatbot used in school, ensuring each rule addresses a specific ethical concern such as honesty, privacy, or inclusivity.
**Assessment Summary:** Students will design a three-point "Fairness Code" and deliver a 2-3 minute presentation pitching this code to the school administration. They must explain how each rule mitigates specific ethical risks in an educational setting.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Proposes three highly distinct rules with compelling justifications that deeply address complex ethical concerns. |
| Meets Expectations | Proposes three clear rules that correctly address specific ethical concerns (honesty, privacy, or inclusivity). |
| Approaching Expectations | Proposes rules that are relevant to ethics but may lack specificity or fail to address distinct concerns clearly. |
| Below Expectations | Fails to propose three rules or the rules do not address the required ethical concerns. |

*   **Skills Demonstrated:** Ethical reasoning, persuasive communication, public speaking.
*   **Evidence of Mastery:** Clear articulation of rules that map directly to specific ethical principles.
*   **Feedback Focus:** Clarity of the rules and the strength of the connection to the ethical concerns.

### Assessment 5: Reflection
**Aligned Objective:** Construct a persuasive paragraph arguing for or against the use of AI in a specific future career field, citing at least two ethical considerations discussed in the course materials.
**Assessment Summary:** Students will choose a career field relevant to their interests and write a single-paragraph persuasive argument. They must take a definitive stance on AI integration in that field and support their position with at least two ethical concepts covered in the course.

| Level | Descriptor |
|-------|------------|
| Exceeds Expectations | Writes a compelling, nuanced argument that seamlessly integrates more than two ethical considerations with strong persuasive language. |
| Meets Expectations | Writes a clear, persuasive paragraph that argues a specific stance and cites exactly two relevant ethical considerations. |
| Approaching Expectations | Writes a paragraph that states a stance but the argument is weak or fails to cite two relevant ethical considerations clearly. |
| Below Expectations | Paragraph lacks a clear stance, is unpersuasive, or does not cite ethical considerations from the course. |

*   **Skills Demonstrated:** Persuasive writing, application of ethical concepts, future planning.
*   **Evidence of Mastery:** Ability to apply course concepts to real-world scenarios in a written format.
*   **Feedback Focus:** Integration of ethical citations and the strength of the persuasive logic.
